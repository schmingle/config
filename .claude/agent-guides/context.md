# Assignment and context selection

Start from the supplied goal, scope, constraints, decisions, approvals, source
locations, and expected result. Use supplied evidence when sufficient; the brief
is a starting point, not an exhaustive context package or a limit on investigation.

Select and read the instructions and evidence relevant to your task. Applicable
repository, domain, testing, and authorization guidance remains mandatory. Trace
callers, dependencies, history, tickets, and linked sources when uncertainty,
complexity, or impact warrants it. Gather additional context with authorized tools;
do not ask the coordinator to perform research you can do yourself.

Check relevant build prerequisites before expensive validation; avoid universal
preflight checklists. Refresh mutable facts before consequential actions. Retrieve
required sources and complete the coverage your role requires before claiming a
conclusion. Report material gaps, unresolved questions, and their effect on the
result. Ask only for
missing decisions, authorization, or access that you cannot resolve independently.
Additional context does not expand the assigned scope or grant new permissions.
