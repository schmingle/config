# Contract quality rubric

Assess these in order so later detail does not conceal an unsound intention or missing decision. The self-contained project contract drives tickets; tickets may demonstrate alignment or actual state, but cannot supply or justify missing project requirements. A contract is not a ticket inventory. Judge substance, not exact headings or the presence of tracking links.

1. **Intention.** Does the Summary clearly explain the problem, purpose, beneficiaries, and desired change within this project's boundary? Question whether the goals address that problem, the reason for pursuing them, and the assumptions that matter to success. Recommend a correction when the intention itself is unsupported or incoherent; do not merely improve its wording.
2. **Consequential decisions.** Are the choices needed to establish the outcome and approach settled, and are assumptions and open questions distinguished from them? A choice is consequential when it changes the outcome, scope, important behavior, a constraint, or a costly commitment. Accept explicit decisions and clear implications of the established plan; plausible guesses are not settled decisions. Leave routine implementation choices to the executor. Missing consequential decisions mean **Gaps found**: name the question and affected work, hold that work, and identify independent ready work. A bounded discovery or decision task may be ready itself; it does not establish that the whole contract is sufficient.
3. **Results and completion proof.** Do the KRs describe observable outcomes that actually fulfill the intention, with concrete before/after scenarios and checks that can demonstrate success? Use numbers only when useful, and do not invent targets. Is there a finite end, with clear boundaries for implementation, deployment, and acceptance as applicable to the chosen scope? Activity, deliverable counts, or merged PRs alone do not prove the intended result.
4. **Plan.** Are the phases a credible route to all KRs, consistent with the consequential decisions? Each phase needs a meaningful result and exit condition. Check dependency order, independent work, and how the main uncertainties will be resolved. A phase must not hide a consequential choice behind implementation detail or require executors to redefine the contract.

Across all four checks, ask whether the contract can be meaningfully shortened without losing essential intent, decisions, outcomes, proof, or approach. Recommend the smallest useful correction. Concision and organization become readiness defects only when their absence obscures intent or creates ambiguity that materially threatens successful execution.
